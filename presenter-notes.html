Act 3 ~90s · 7 clicks — don't rush this one

"How a checkpoint works." — running total (Flink's memory) + a growing list of saved checkpoints
① growing total
"Right now, this number only exists in one place — in memory, on one machine. If that machine goes down, this number goes with it."
② first checkpoint
"Flink just copied that exact number somewhere durable. That's called a checkpoint, and it happens automatically, every few seconds, without anyone doing anything."
③ growing again
"Nothing paused — the data kept moving the entire time that checkpoint was being saved."
④ second checkpoint
→ point at the list
"There's a second checkpoint now. Flink just keeps doing this, continuously, in the background."
⑤ crash
"Now here's the failure case: the worker holding this number just crashed."
⑥ restore
"Flink doesn't start over from zero — it goes and reloads the last checkpoint it saved."
⑦ resume — exactly-once
"That's the whole point: nothing before the checkpoint is lost, and nothing after it gets counted twice. That's what 'exactly-once' actually means."

Act 4 ~50s · 5 clicks

"Scaling maps to shards." — shard/subtask lane pairs + KPU count
① 2 shards / 2 subtasks
"Two shards on the stream, two parallel subtasks in Flink, two KPUs behind them — that's a one-to-one relationship."
② traffic picks up
"Now say traffic on this stream picks up."
③ 3rd subtask + KPU appears
"Flink just added a third subtask and a third KPU to handle it — and nobody on our team provisioned anything by hand."
④ auto-scaling framing
"That's what auto-scaling actually means here: more compute behind the scenes, not more servers for us to manage."
⑤ resharding lever
"There's one thing that's still on us, though: parallelism can't go higher than the number of shards. If we need more than that, we have to reshard the stream ourselves."

Act 5 ~50s · 5 clicks

"One stream in, a new stream out." — raw stream forks to "other consumers" and to Flink → new output stream
① raw stream untouched
"The raw stream keeps flowing exactly like it did before — anyone else who needs those raw events can still read them directly."
② Flink computes something new
"Flink is reading that same stream, and continuously computing something new out of it."
③ pace mismatch
→ point at the two speeds
"Notice how much less often something comes out on the right compared to what's going in on the left — that's aggregation."
④ two streams, two audiences
"So now there are two streams — the raw one, and a brand-new one that only exists for this result."
⑤ the SQL statement
"In Flink SQL, that's really just one statement: insert into the new stream, select from the raw one."

If you lose your place — 3 things to land no matter what

  1. Flink processes continuously, one record at a time — never in batches.
  2. Checkpoints are why a crash costs seconds, not the whole job — that's "exactly-once."
  3. Scaling maps to shards, and AWS provisions the compute (KPUs) automatically — you don't manage servers.
